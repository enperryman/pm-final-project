Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Problem Hook & Value Proposition, Module 1

  • Scenario: RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)

Strategic crisis

The company will lose the largest enterprise account and potentially other accounts due to the opened gap.

Moment of misery

They are testing alternate solutions.

Problem hook

We must solve for the loss of business by addressing the increased administrative burden to better support frontline usefulness.

Value proposition

For the 95% of users who rely on RouteLogic daily, we will remove extra layers because we are at risk of losing enterprise accounts to competitors.

Cold-read self-review

The business risk is the loss of executive accounts. It is a systemic problem because our clients are actively trying alternate solutions. It is removing obstacles by focusing on the needs of the majority of users.

Experimentation Plan

Parameters

Parameter · Decision

Feature under test · One-Click Compliance Checklist: Collapse the 14.6-min compliance step into a smart pre-filled form

Persona · Driver

Expected outcome · The driver will no longer exit the delivery workflow application to notify dispatch the delivery status, simplifying the log compliance checks step and increasing usage and decreasing time spent.

Primary success metric · Time to Log Compliance Checks

Baseline rate · 14.6 minutes

Guardrail metric · Manager Satisfaction in Reporting

Guardrail boundary · must not drop more than 10%

Second guardrail · ·

Minimum Detectable Effect · 5

Sample size per arm · 1,251

Traffic split · 50/50

Test duration · 2 weekly cycles

Significance threshold · p < 0.05 (95%)

Control vs. Variant

  • Control (A): Drivers are exiting the app and texting dispatch instead.
  • Variant (B): One-Click Compliance Checklist

Entry point — Active stop --> Feature core — Delivery and checklist --> Success/confirmation

The active stop, required checklist items, and delivery action must appear together on the feature core screen.

The completion action must submit delivery and checklist completion together, with one driver action.

The completion action must remain disabled until all required items have explicit driver confirmation.

The interface must never pre-check or auto-confirm a required compliance item on the driver’s behalf.

After submission, the prototype must show either a confirmation that both records were saved or an error that says they were not recorded.

On a simulated submission failure, the driver’s current checklist selections must remain visible and a retry action must be available.

The success state must identify the stop and separately confirm delivery status and compliance-check status.

Prototype evaluation should test whether a driver can complete the flow in 7.3 minutes or less. This is a proposed high-fidelity prototype target: half of the referenced 14.6-minute compliance step, not an agreed production commitment.

  • Held constant (isolation check): The remaining application is the same for all experiences.
5 · Module 6
GTM Launch Plan & Success Dashboard

GTM Launch Plan, RouteLogic (B2B)

Field · Value

Feature · One-Click Compliance Checklist

Goal · Engagement

Launch tier · M, Targeted

Goal & Audience

  • Goal: Engagement, Drivers are currently exiting the workflow application to notify dispatch delivery statuses because the step that should take no more than 3 minutes is taking an average of 14.6 min. We want to deepen the engagement from the drivers.
  • Target audience: Drivers

Launch Tier

  • M, Targeted, Existing feature is being updated to improve user experience. The launch requires proactive communication to a targeted audience and a focused GTM plan. A L multi-channel and full-scale XL campaign are not warranted, but a targeted focused approach is essential to connect with the drivers and increase adoption.

Channels

1. Owned: Email: Targeted email to reach drivers introducing the upgraded feature reducing the compliance log step to On-Click reducing the time to complete and need to use communication outside of the application. Second role-specific email to managers or dispatch to notify of the upgrade and encourage adoption.

2. Owned: Push Notifications: Reaching drivers already using the application to describe the new One-Click Compliance Checklist with a deep-link to the updated workflow. .

3. Owned: In-product demo: Reaching drivers within the app with clear short-form video demonstration of the new feature experience within the existing workflow.

Enablement & Assets

Launch emails announcing the upgraded On-Click Compliance Checklist Feature with launch date.

Push Notification sequence introducing the upgraded feature.

Short-form video demonstrating the new feature experience.

Ownership, Budget & Timeline

  • Ownership & budget: PM: Owns the launch plan, coordinates all teams, and monitors post-launch metrics.

Marketing: Launch email.

Design: Owns the demo video.

Engineering: Owns push notification, in-product demo, and performance monitoring on launch day.

Customer Support: Briefed on One-Click Compliance Checklist before launch.

No additional budget required.

  • Timeline: Week 1: Launch Emails with Launch Date

Week 2: Launch day + push notification + in-product demo

Week 3+: Monitor, collect feedback, iterate

Success Metrics

  • Metrics: Checklist completion time: median elapsed time from opening the eligible compliance step to successful submission of the required checklist.

14-day driver adoption: percentage of eligible drivers who successfully complete at least one checklist using the upgraded workflow within 14 days of becoming eligible.

  • Bad signal to watch for: Daily Active Users but One-Click Compliance Checklist is not being used
  • Likely post-launch decision: Iterate to adjust the messaging/channel because data is showing mixed signals.
Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

Setting up the testing was the hardest for me to fully understand. I walked through multiple scenarios to help establish a better foundation for this particular phase.

Aha moment

My aha moment was the importance of selecting the correct metrics to determine success. The bad signals can look good, but will not tell the full story.

Key learnings

Understanding the appropriate metrics to "chase" when identifying solutions and what key features will provide the appropriate solution for the pain points.
